01Choosing in ten seconds

The three papers in the shop are not good, better and best. They are three different characters, and the right one depends on what the piece has to do.

02MultiArt Silk — coated, bright white

The workhorse. A silk-coated, bright white sheet that holds colour exactly where you put it.

How it feels

Smooth and cool, with a very slight sheen. A silk coating sits between gloss and matt: it is not shiny, it does not throw a reflection back at you under a ceiling light, but you can feel that the surface has been closed. Run a nail across it and there is no tooth.

What it does to your ink

The coating stops the paper from drinking. Ink sits on top and dries there, so colours come out close to full strength, blacks are dense rather than grey, and 6 pt type stays legible instead of blurring at the edges. If your design leans on photography, a gradient or a large flat colour, this is the sheet that will show it.

Where it works

Flyers with images. Posters that have to read across a room. Business cards that need punch. Anything where the print is the point and the paper should stay out of the way.

What to watch

It is not a writing paper — a ballpoint skates on it and ink pens smudge. On the heavy weights, a large dark solid will show fingerprints. And bright white is genuinely bright: on screen a warm photograph can look warmer than it will on this sheet.

Provenance

Wood-free coated, FSC-certified.

03Biotop — uncoated, warm natural

The quiet one. Uncoated, faintly cream, and the only sheet here you can comfortably write on.

How it feels

Dry and slightly warm, with a faint tooth you can feel under a fingertip. The shade is a natural off-white — it has not been bleached blue-white, so it reads warm next to a MultiArt sheet. It is also bulky for its weight: a 300 gsm Biotop card feels thicker than a 300 gsm coated one.

What it does to your ink

Uncoated paper absorbs. Colours settle a step quieter, blacks read as a deep charcoal rather than jet, and very fine detail softens a fraction as the ink spreads into the fibres. What you get back is a page that does not glare, is easy on the eye at reading distance, and looks made rather than manufactured.

Where it works

Text-led flyers. Invitations and wedding stationery. Letterheads. Business cards that will be written on — a name, a date, an appointment. Anything with a warm or editorial feel.

What to watch

Do not expect a photograph to pop. A large dark solid will look softer and less even than it does on screen, and the warm base shade tints everything very slightly — pale greys pick up a little cream, and there is no such thing as a brilliant white here.

Provenance

A totally chlorine-free (TCF) uncoated paper, carrying the EU Ecolabel and FSC certification.

04RecyStar — 100 % recycled, unbleached

The honest one. Made entirely from recovered paper, left unbleached, and it does not pretend otherwise.

How it feels

Close to Biotop in the hand — uncoated, dry, a little tooth — but greyer and warmer in shade, with tiny flecks of the fibres it was made from scattered through the sheet. Those flecks are the point. They are what tells someone, without a logo or a claim, that the paper had a life before this one.

What it does to your ink

It absorbs like Biotop, and then the darker base shade lowers everything by another step. The same file printed on MultiArt Silk and on RecyStar will look like two different designs: on RecyStar the whole range is compressed, quieter and softer, with less distance between the lightest and the darkest area.

Where it works

Anything where the material is part of the message. Zines and posters with a raw look, cards for makers and studios, and every job where the recycled content matters more than the punch.

What to watch

There is no white ink. Every area you leave white in your design comes out the colour of the paper — a warm grey — so a design built on crisp white space will read differently. Shade also varies a little from batch to batch, which is normal for a recycled stock and not a defect.

Provenance

100 % recycled fibre, unbleached, carrying the Blue Angel and EU Ecolabel marks.

05What the weight means

Paper is sold by grammage — grams per square metre. It is a weight, not a thickness, but for a given family it works as one: the higher the number, the stiffer the sheet.

WeightFeels likeUse it for
115 – 135 gsmA magazine page. Bends easily, stacks flat. Flyers you hand out by the hundred
160 – 170 gsmNoticeably stiffer. Holds its shape when held at one corner. Posters, and flyers meant to survive a pocket
250 gsmA light card. Stands up on its own. Invitations, loyalty cards, a card on a budget
300 gsmThe standard business-card weight. Business cards, invitations, anything that should feel solid
350 – 400 gsmThick. Makes a sound when it lands on a table. Cards that have to be remembered

Because Biotop and RecyStar are uncoated and bulky, they feel thicker than a coated sheet of the same weight. A 300 gsm Biotop card and a 350 gsm MultiArt Silk card are close cousins in the hand.

06Colour, and why the sheet changes it

A screen is a lamp: it makes colour by shining light at you. Paper is not — it can only reflect the light already in the room. That difference alone means a print will never be an exact copy of what you approved on a monitor, and it is not a fault.

The paper then adds its own accent on top. The same file on our three stocks gives three results: brightest and most contrasted on MultiArt Silk, a step quieter and warmer on Biotop, quieter and greyer again on RecyStar.

  • We print CMYK. Very saturated screen colours — electric blues, fluorescent greens — sit outside what four inks can reach and will come back a little more muted.
  • There is no white ink. White in your artwork means "leave the paper bare", so on Biotop it is cream and on RecyStar it is warm grey. Except on a coloured paper — there we can print white ink.
  • Large flat solids show the paper most. If a design is one big colour field, choose the stock deliberately.

07Papers we keep, but do not sell online

Colorplan

GF Smith's coloured paper, made in the UK, in around fifty solid colours from 135 to 700 gsm. The colour is dyed right through the sheet, so a cut edge or a fold shows colour and not a white core — which is why it is the standard choice for a serious business card, an invitation or a cover. Two sheets can be bonded together for a card with a coloured edge.
