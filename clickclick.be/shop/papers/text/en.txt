[choose]  Choosing in ten seconds

<p class="lede">The three papers in the shop are not good, better and best. They are three
different characters, and the right one depends on what the piece has to do.</p>
<div class="tw"><table>
<tr><th>If you want</th><th>Take</th><th>Because</th></tr>
<tr><td>Photographs, deep blacks, precise colour</td><td><strong>MultiArt Silk</strong></td>
    <td>It is coated, so the ink stays on the surface instead of sinking in</td></tr>
<tr><td>A warm, natural page you can write on</td><td><strong>Biotop</strong></td>
    <td>Uncoated and slightly cream — a pen, a stamp or a signature all work on it</td></tr>
<tr><td>The lowest footprint, with visible character</td><td><strong>RecyStar</strong></td>
    <td>100&nbsp;% recycled and unbleached; you can see the fibres it was made from</td></tr>
</table></div>

[multiart]  MultiArt Silk — coated, bright white

<p class="lede">The workhorse. A silk-coated, bright white sheet that holds colour exactly
where you put it.</p>
{photos multiart-silk | The blank sheet | The same stock, printed}
<h3>How it feels</h3>
<p>Smooth and cool, with a very slight sheen. A silk coating sits between gloss and matt: it is
not shiny, it does not throw a reflection back at you under a ceiling light, but you can feel that
the surface has been closed. Run a nail across it and there is no tooth.</p>

<h3>What it does to your ink</h3>
<p>The coating stops the paper from drinking. Ink sits on top and dries there, so colours come out
close to full strength, blacks are dense rather than grey, and 6&nbsp;pt type stays legible instead
of blurring at the edges. If your design leans on photography, a gradient or a large flat colour,
this is the sheet that will show it.</p>

<h3>Where it works</h3>
<p>Flyers with images. Posters that have to read across a room. Business cards that need punch.
Anything where the print is the point and the paper should stay out of the way.</p>

<h3>What to watch</h3>
<p>It is not a writing paper — a ballpoint skates on it and ink pens smudge. On the heavy weights,
a large dark solid will show fingerprints. And bright white is genuinely bright: on screen a warm
photograph can look warmer than it will on this sheet.</p>

<h3>Provenance</h3>
<p>Wood-free coated, FSC-certified.</p>

# --------------------------------------------------------------------------
[biotop]  Biotop — uncoated, warm natural

<p class="lede">The quiet one. Uncoated, faintly cream, and the only sheet here you can
comfortably write on.</p>
{photos biotop | The blank sheet | The same stock, printed}
<h3>How it feels</h3>
<p>Dry and slightly warm, with a faint tooth you can feel under a fingertip. The shade is a natural
off-white — it has not been bleached blue-white, so it reads warm next to a MultiArt sheet. It is
also bulky for its weight: a 300&nbsp;gsm Biotop card feels thicker than a 300&nbsp;gsm coated one.</p>

<h3>What it does to your ink</h3>
<p>Uncoated paper absorbs. Colours settle a step quieter, blacks read as a deep charcoal rather
than jet, and very fine detail softens a fraction as the ink spreads into the fibres. What you get
back is a page that does not glare, is easy on the eye at reading distance, and looks made rather
than manufactured.</p>

<h3>Where it works</h3>
<p>Text-led flyers. Invitations and wedding stationery. Letterheads. Business cards that will be
written on — a name, a date, an appointment. Anything with a warm or editorial feel.</p>

<h3>What to watch</h3>
<p>Do not expect a photograph to pop. A large dark solid will look softer and less even than it
does on screen, and the warm base shade tints everything very slightly — pale greys pick up a
little cream, and there is no such thing as a brilliant white here.</p>

<h3>Provenance</h3>
<p>A totally chlorine-free (TCF) uncoated paper, carrying the EU Ecolabel and FSC
certification.</p>

# --------------------------------------------------------------------------
[recystar]  RecyStar — 100 % recycled, unbleached

<p class="lede">The honest one. Made entirely from recovered paper, left unbleached, and it
does not pretend otherwise.</p>
{photos recystar | The blank sheet | The same stock, printed}
<h3>How it feels</h3>
<p>Close to Biotop in the hand — uncoated, dry, a little tooth — but greyer and warmer in shade,
with tiny flecks of the fibres it was made from scattered through the sheet. Those flecks are the
point. They are what tells someone, without a logo or a claim, that the paper had a life before
this one.</p>

<h3>What it does to your ink</h3>
<p>It absorbs like Biotop, and then the darker base shade lowers everything by another step. The
same file printed on MultiArt Silk and on RecyStar will look like two different designs: on
RecyStar the whole range is compressed, quieter and softer, with less distance between the
lightest and the darkest area.</p>

<h3>Where it works</h3>
<p>Anything where the material is part of the message. Zines and posters with a raw look, cards for
makers and studios, and every job where the recycled content matters more than the punch.</p>

<h3>What to watch</h3>
<p>There is no white ink. Every area you leave white in your design comes out the colour of the
paper — a warm grey — so a design built on crisp white space will read differently. Shade also
varies a little from batch to batch, which is normal for a recycled stock and not a defect.</p>

<h3>Provenance</h3>
<p>100&nbsp;% recycled fibre, unbleached, carrying the Blue Angel and EU Ecolabel marks.</p>

[gsm]  What the weight means

<p>Paper is sold by grammage — grams per square metre. It is a weight, not a thickness, but for a
given family it works as one: the higher the number, the stiffer the sheet.</p>
<div class="tw"><table>
<tr><th>Weight</th><th>Feels like</th><th>Use it for</th></tr>
<tr><td>115 – 135 gsm</td><td>A magazine page. Bends easily, stacks flat.</td>
    <td>Flyers you hand out by the hundred</td></tr>
<tr><td>160 – 170 gsm</td><td>Noticeably stiffer. Holds its shape when held at one corner.</td>
    <td>Posters, and flyers meant to survive a pocket</td></tr>
<tr><td>250 gsm</td><td>A light card. Stands up on its own.</td>
    <td>Invitations, loyalty cards, a card on a budget</td></tr>
<tr><td>300 gsm</td><td>The standard business-card weight.</td>
    <td>Business cards, invitations, anything that should feel solid</td></tr>
<tr><td>350 – 400 gsm</td><td>Thick. Makes a sound when it lands on a table.</td>
    <td>Cards that have to be remembered</td></tr>
</table></div>
<p>Because Biotop and RecyStar are uncoated and bulky, they feel thicker than a coated sheet of the
same weight. A 300&nbsp;gsm Biotop card and a 350&nbsp;gsm MultiArt Silk card are close cousins in
the hand.</p>

# --------------------------------------------------------------------------
[colour]  Colour, and why the sheet changes it

<p>A screen is a lamp: it makes colour by shining light at you. Paper is not — it can only reflect
the light already in the room. That difference alone means a print will never be an exact copy of
what you approved on a monitor, and it is not a fault.</p>
<p>The paper then adds its own accent on top. The same file on our three stocks gives three
results: brightest and most contrasted on MultiArt Silk, a step quieter and warmer on Biotop,
quieter and greyer again on RecyStar.</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>We print CMYK.</strong> Very saturated screen colours — electric blues, fluorescent
      greens — sit outside what four inks can reach and will come back a little more muted.</li>
  <li><strong>There is no white ink.</strong> White in your artwork means "leave the paper bare",
      so on Biotop it is cream and on RecyStar it is warm grey. Except on a coloured paper — there
      we can print white ink.</li>
  <li><strong>Large flat solids show the paper most.</strong> If a design is one big colour field,
      choose the stock deliberately.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Colorplan</h3>
{photos colorplan | The blank sheet | The same stock, printed}
<p>GF Smith's coloured paper, made in the UK, in around fifty solid colours from 135 to
700&nbsp;gsm. The colour is dyed right through the sheet, so a cut edge or a fold shows colour and
not a white core — which is why it is the standard choice for a serious business card, an
invitation or a cover. Two sheets can be bonded together for a card with a coloured edge.</p>

<h3>The Fedrigoni collection</h3>
